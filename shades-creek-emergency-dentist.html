Broken & Cracked Teeth

Broken, Cracked & Fractured Tooth Repair

A break can expose the sensitive inner layers of the tooth, leave a sharp edge that cuts your tongue, or run deep enough to reach the nerve. What we can save depends heavily on how quickly you are seen — a small crack is a filling or a crown, while the same crack left alone can become a root canal or an extraction.

  • Digital X-rays and 3D imaging to see how far the fracture goes
  • Bonding or a filling for smaller chips and fractures
  • A same-day CEREC crown when the tooth needs full coverage
  • Root canal therapy if the fracture has reached the nerve

Crown Emergencies

Crown Fell Out, Broke, or Came Loose?

A crown that has come off leaves the prepared tooth underneath completely unprotected — it is sensitive to air and temperature, it can shift within days, and decay can start where the margin is exposed. Do not glue it back yourself. Bring it in.

  • Intact crown and healthy tooth? Often re-cemented the same day
  • Broken crown or decay underneath? A new CEREC crown, milled in-house
  • Digital scanning — no gooey impression trays
  • Porcelain shade-matched to the teeth beside it

Urgent Root Canal

Emergency Root Canal Therapy

Throbbing that keeps you up at night, pain on hot or cold that lingers, a bad taste, a pimple on the gum, facial swelling — those are signs the nerve inside the tooth is infected or dying. A root canal removes the infection and saves the tooth. It is also what stops the pain.

  • Root canal therapy performed here, by Dr. MacBeth
  • 3D cone beam imaging to map the canals before we start
  • Infection and abscess treatment — swelling addressed promptly
  • Followed by a crown to protect the tooth long-term

When a Tooth Can't Be Saved

Bridges, Partials & Full Dentures

Sometimes the honest answer is that a tooth is beyond saving. When that happens you will hear it plainly, along with every option for replacing it — because the gap left behind changes how you chew, how neighbouring teeth drift, and how the jawbone holds up over time.

  • Fixed porcelain bridges — fills the gap using the teeth either side, nothing to take out
  • Partial dentures — replaces several teeth while keeping the healthy ones you have
  • Full dentures — a complete arch restored, fitted for comfort and appearance
  • Dental implants — the closest thing to the tooth you lost; won't slip, click or shift

Before You Arrive

First-Aid Tips for Common Emergencies

While getting professional care is always the priority, a few simple steps can help protect your tooth and manage discomfort until you reach us.

For a knocked-out toothHandle it by the crown, not the root, and gently rinse it if dirty. If possible, place it back in the socket or keep it in milk, and call us immediately. Time is critical.
For a severe toothacheRinse with warm water and gently floss to remove any trapped debris. A cold compress on the outside of the cheek can help with swelling.
For a broken or chipped toothSave any pieces if you can, rinse your mouth with warm water, and apply a cold compress to reduce swelling.
For a lost filling or crownKeep the area clean and, if you still have the crown, keep it safe to bring with you.
For swelling or an abscessRinse with warm salt water and contact us promptly, as infections need timely attention.

Always call us for guidance tailored to your specific situation. These tips are meant to help in the moment, not to replace professional care.

Do Not Ignore Dental Pain

It can be tempting to wait out a toothache or put off dealing with a chipped tooth, but delaying care often makes things worse. What starts as minor discomfort can develop into a serious infection, and a small crack can grow into a break that threatens the tooth.

Prompt treatment not only relieves your pain faster but often allows for simpler, more conservative care and a better chance of saving your natural tooth. If you are in pain or something feels wrong, trust your instincts and reach out.

Preventing Future Dental Emergencies

While not every emergency can be avoided, many can be prevented with a few smart habits. Keeping up with regular checkups allows us to catch and treat small problems before they become painful emergencies.

Practicing good oral hygiene protects against decay and infection, and wearing a mouthguard during sports helps prevent injuries. It is also wise to avoid using your teeth as tools and to steer clear of chewing hard objects like ice.
