Not Every Dental Issue Can Wait for a Routine Appointment

A true dental emergency generally involves severe pain, uncontrolled bleeding, a risk of infection spreading, or trauma that affects your ability to eat, speak, or function normally. Waiting even a day or two can mean the difference between saving a tooth and losing it — or between quick, straightforward treatment and a more complex, costly procedure down the road.

Understanding what qualifies as urgent helps you make the right call when a dental issue suddenly arises, rather than ignoring a serious problem or feeling unsure whether a same-day visit is warranted.

Common Dental Emergencies We Treat

From a sudden toothache to a knocked-out tooth, these are the urgent cases we see most — and treat same-day whenever possible.

Severe or Persistent Tooth Pain

Intense pain that comes on suddenly or won't subside often signals a deeper infection or nerve involvement that needs prompt evaluation. Call — don't wait for it to worsen.

Knocked-Out Tooth

Acting within the first hour gives the best chance of saving and re-implanting the tooth. Handle it by the crown, keep it moist in milk, and call us right away.

Cracked or Broken Tooth

Depending on severity, a break can expose sensitive inner layers or nerve tissue. Save any pieces, rinse with warm water, and call us the same day to prevent infection.

Lost Filling or Crown

An exposed tooth without its protective covering is vulnerable to further damage and increased sensitivity. Keep the area clean, avoid chewing on that side, and call to get it restored.

Swelling or Dental Abscess

Facial swelling, especially with fever or difficulty swallowing, can indicate a spreading infection that requires urgent attention. Call right away.

Soft Tissue Injuries

Cuts or trauma to the lips, gums, tongue, or cheeks from an accident or dental-related injury may require prompt evaluation and care.

Broken Braces or Appliance

A wire or bracket causing pain or irritation — or broken in a way that affects treatment — should be addressed quickly so your orthodontic progress stays on track.

What Happens During an Emergency Visit

Emergency visits are structured to move quickly from diagnosis to treatment, minimizing the time you spend in pain or uncertainty.

01

Immediate Evaluation

The affected tooth or area is examined right away, often with X-rays, to determine the exact cause and extent.

02

Pain Management

Prompt relief — local anesthesia, medication, or addressing the source of discomfort directly.

03

Diagnosis & Planning

A clear explanation of the issue plus the recommended treatment: filling, extraction, root canal or otherwise.

04

Same-Day Treatment

Stabilizing a knocked-out tooth, treating an infection, or repairing a broken restoration — often the same day.

05

Follow-Up Planning

If more treatment is needed, you leave with a clear plan and timeline to fully resolve the underlying issue.

Don't Wait It Out

Why Timing Matters in a Dental Emergency

~60 min

The knocked-out tooth window

A knocked-out tooth has a much higher chance of being saved if it is re-implanted within about an hour — and that window narrows quickly with each passing hour.

Spreads

Infection doesn't stay put

An untreated abscess doesn't simply stay contained. It can spread to surrounding teeth and gum tissue and, in more serious cases, deeper into the jaw or bloodstream.

Listen

Pain is a signal

Severe or worsening tooth pain rarely resolves on its own. It usually means a deep infection or significant fracture is actively getting worse, not better.

Many dental emergencies involve a window of time where prompt treatment significantly improves the outcome. Addressing these issues quickly not only relieves discomfort faster, it frequently allows for a less invasive and less costly treatment than what may be needed if the same issue is left untreated for days or weeks.
