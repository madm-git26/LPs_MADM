# Planning numbers for a dental account

**These are planning assumptions, not published benchmarks.** They exist so a new
account has somewhere to start before it has its own data. Replace every one of
them with the practice's actual numbers as soon as there are thirty conversions
to look at - a single practice's real CPA beats any range on this page.

Costs vary enormously by market: a competitive metro can run three to five times
a small town for the same search.

## Where the numbers come from

Ask the practice, in this order:

1. **Average revenue per new patient**, by service line - their practice
   management software knows this exactly.
2. **Show rate** - what share of booked new patients actually arrive.
3. **Case acceptance** - what share of proposed treatment gets done.
4. **Chair capacity** - how many new patients they can absorb per month. An
   account that books more patients than the practice can see is not a success.

Then: `acceptable CPA = new patient value × acceptable acquisition share ×
show rate`. Skipping the show rate is the most common way a dental CPA target
ends up 30% too generous.

## Typical shape of a healthy local dental account

| Signal | What "healthy" tends to look like | What it means when it is off |
|---|---|---|
| Share of conversions that are calls | Most of them, often the large majority | Very few calls usually means the number is hard to find on mobile |
| Qualified call rate (60s+) | Comfortably over half | Below 40%: wrong-number traffic, price shoppers, or an unanswered phone |
| Mobile share of emergency traffic | The overwhelming majority | Desktop-heavy emergency traffic is a targeting problem |
| Search impression share | 60%+ on brand and core "near me" terms | Below that on brand, someone is bidding on you |
| Lost IS (rank) vs (budget) | Both under 20% | Rank loss = bids/quality; budget loss = money. Opposite fixes |
| Cost per booked appointment vs cost per call | Booked should be 1.5-3× the call cost | A much bigger gap means calls are not converting at the desk |

## Relative cost by service line

Rather than dollar figures that will be wrong for your market, the ordering is
stable almost everywhere:

```
emergency  <  general/family  <  cosmetic  <  ortho  <  implants
```

Implant and "full mouth" terms are usually the most expensive clicks in the
account by a wide margin, which is exactly why they get income and postal-code
targeting while emergency does not.

## Sanity checks that catch real problems

- **Conversions ≈ 3× the practice's new-patient count?** The account is counting
  something that is not a patient.
- **CPA improving while new patients fall?** Almost always a tracking change, not
  a performance gain.
- **Emergency CPA below general CPA?** Normal - and a good reason to fund
  emergency first.
- **Zero conversions on a keyword with 200 clicks?** That is not bad luck.
- **Weekend spend with no weekend hours?** Money into voicemail.

## Seasonality worth planning for

January (new insurance year, deductibles reset), late spring (pre-summer
cosmetic), and December (use-it-or-lose-it benefits) run hot in most US markets.
Mid-summer is usually soft. Budget for it rather than reacting to it - and expect
January CPCs to be the year's highest.
